MSA Altair 4XR — Monthly Maintenance
Equipment & Cal Gas
  • 4-gas calibration cylinder — 1.45% CH4 (60% LEL) · 15.0% O2 · 60 ppm CO · 20 ppm H2S
  • 0.25 L/min fixed-flow regulator · Calibration cap + tubing
  • Verify cylinder is not expired and screen gas values match the cylinder label before flowing gas.
  • Perform all tests in clean, fresh air — away from apparatus exhaust and fuel vapors.
1. Zero (Fresh Air Setup)E4454: Monthly · Clean air only
  1. Turn unit ON (hold center button) in fresh air. Allow self-check to finish and readings to stabilize (O2 20.8%, others at 0). Checkmark indicates completion.
  2. Press and hold the ▲ (up) button for 3 seconds.
  3. Screen shows ZERO CAL? — press the center (OK) button to confirm.
  4. Unit zeros CO / H2S / LEL and sets O2 to fresh-air baseline. Keep it in clean air and don't breathe on the sensors.
  5. When zero completes, the unit prompts SPAN CAL? — continue to Section 2 to calibrate, or let it time out to return to normal operation.
2. Span CalibrationE4454: Monthly · After zero / failed bump
  1. From the SPAN CAL? prompt (follows a completed zero), press the center (OK) button to confirm.
  2. Attach the calibration cap, connect tubing to the regulator, and open the valve to flow gas.
  3. Unit runs span automatically (about 1–2 minutes). Leave gas flowing until it reports the result.
  4. When complete, close the regulator valve and remove the calibration cap.
  5. Sensors that fail span are left unchanged — the unit will flag them. A unit with a failed sensor is out of service: tag it and report it.
3. Bump TestE4454: Monthly
  1. With the unit ON and readings stable in fresh air, press the ▼ (down) button. Screen shows BUMP TEST?
  2. Press the center (OK) button to confirm.
  3. Attach calibration cap to the unit, connect tubing, and open the regulator to flow gas.
  4. Wait for the unit to complete the test. PASS: a ✓ checkmark displays in measure mode for 24 hours.
  5. Close the regulator valve and remove the calibration cap. Readings return to normal.
  6. FAIL: do NOT use the unit — perform a full zero + span calibration (Sections 1–2), then re-bump. If it still fails, tag it out.
E4454 Schedule & Pass Criteria
  • Zero, span calibration, and bump test: once a month — and any time the unit takes a hard hit or heavy smoke exposure.
  • Span calibrate immediately after any failed bump test.
  • Combustible sensor accuracy must read 0 to +20% of applied gas — outside that range, calibrate before use.
  • Clear the peak readings after each use so the next crew starts with a clean slate.
  • Manufacturer's recommendation is a bump test before each day's use — follow current department SOP if it differs.

Troubleshooting — Problem → ActionIf it can't pass a test, it doesn't ride
Fails ZEROMove to truly clean air — no exhaust, fuel vapors, or smoke — and retry. Don't breathe on the sensors. If it still fails, run a full span calibration; repeated zero failures mean a drifting or exhausted sensor.
Fails SPAN CALCheck cylinder expiration date and pressure. Verify the regulator is the 0.25 L/min fixed-flow type, tubing isn't cracked or kinked, and the cal cap is fully seated on the case. Retry with known-good gas; if a sensor still fails, tag the unit out.
Fails BUMPRecalibrate (zero + span), then re-bump. A bump failure right after a good calibration means the sensor can't respond — replace that sensor or send the unit for service.
Slow or weak responseInspect sensor inlets and dust/water filters for dirt, soot, or moisture blockage — common after fire assignments. Wipe the housing with a damp cloth only: no solvents, no compressed air into the sensor ports.
O2 off in fresh airFresh air should read 20.8%. If it doesn't, zero the unit in clean air. Persistent drift means an aging O2 sensor — expect to replace it. O2 sensors wear out from normal use even when the unit sits on the charger.
LEL reads low or erraticCombustible sensor may be poisoned (silicones, sulfur compounds, leaded fuel) or desensitized after heavy exposure. Bump immediately after any suspected exposure; calibrate or replace the sensor if it can't pass.
Won't power on / short runtimeCharge fully and retry. Cold weather cuts runtime noticeably — keep the unit warm in winter and off the apparatus floor. If it won't hold a charge, battery service is required.
SENSOR ERROR / missing sensorCycle power once. If the error returns, the unit can't read that sensor — out of service, tag and report. Don't run a 4-gas with a dead channel.
False alarms in cab / on sceneDiesel exhaust, fueling operations, and cleaning products can legitimately trip CO or LEL. Confirm in fresh air before writing the unit off as faulty — if readings clear in clean air, the detector did its job.
Alarms won't clearMove to fresh air and give the sensors time to purge — high exposures take longer to clear. If readings show zero but the alarm persists, cycle power. STEL/TWA alarms reflect accumulated exposure and reset on restart.
Prevention — Keep It Off This Sheet
  • Keep the unit on the charger between shifts — the weekly charge check catches a dead battery before a call does.
  • Sensors are consumables. They wear out with age and exposure — frequent cal failures mean replacement time.
  • After heavy smoke or a dusty assignment, check the filters and run a bump even if one isn't due.
  • Never clean with solvents or silicone-based products — they poison the LEL sensor. Damp cloth only.
